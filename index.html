Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Learn more about the NED polymer coil technology, clinical evidence, and availability.

What is the NED polymer coil and how does it differ from traditional embolic coils?

The NED is a polymer-based embolic coil constructed from a proprietary radiopaque shape-memory biocompatible polymer. Unlike traditional metal coils that rely primarily on mechanical packing to achieve occlusion, the NED coil achieves vascular occlusion through the intrinsic material properties of the polymer itself. This results in higher pack density, faster occlusion times, and — most importantly — minimal CT and MRI artifact, enabling clear post-procedural imaging that metal coils simply cannot provide.

What is the FDA clearance status of the NED coil?

The NED polymer coil received FDA 510(k) clearance in May 2025 for use in obstructing or reducing blood flow in the peripheral vasculature. The device shares the same intended use as the Boston Scientific Interlock-18 Detachable Coil (IDC), which served as the predicate device.

What indications has the NED coil been used for in clinical practice?

During the limited market release, the NED coil has been used across four distinct indication types: pelvic congestion syndrome, rectal artery embolization, varicocele embolization, and DVA with venous embolization. Cases have included both arterial and venous embolization in patients ranging from 21 to 80 years of age.

What clinical results has the NED coil achieved so far?

As of March 2026, the NED polymer coil has achieved a 100% technical success rate across 11 cases at 4 institutions, treating 31 vessel sites with only 73 coils — an average of just 2.4 coils per treatment site. Pre-clinical GLP data further supports this, showing +11.5% higher pack density, 19% faster occlusion, and 23% fewer coils compared to the predicate device.

Why is minimal imaging artifact a game-changer?

For decades, physicians have accepted that coil embolization means losing visibility on follow-up imaging. Metal coils create dense starburst artifacts on CT and MRI that obscure surrounding anatomy, making it difficult to assess treatment success, detect complications, or plan additional interventions. The NED polymer coil changes this fundamental trade-off — producing minimal artifact so physicians can clearly visualize the treated vessel and surrounding tissue.

What sizes are available in the NED product portfolio?

The NED product line currently includes five SKUs designed for both framing and packing applications. Framing coils are available for vessel diameters of 2–3 mm, 3–4 mm, and 4–5 mm. Packing coils cover vessel diameters of 2–3 mm and 3–5 mm. Coil lengths range from 3.5 cm to 16 cm depending on the model.

Is the NED coil compatible with standard delivery systems?

The NED coil is designed for delivery through standard microcatheters used in peripheral embolization procedures. It is deployed using a detachment mechanism compatible with existing interventional radiology workflows, requiring no specialized equipment beyond what is already available in most catheterization labs.

Caution

Important Safety Information

Federal law (USA) restricts this device to sale by or on the order of a licensed practitioner. Prior to use, please refer to all applicable Instructions for Use for more information on intended use, indications, contraindications, warnings, precautions, potential adverse events, and operator's instructions.

Intended use / indications for use

The NED embolic coil is indicated to obstruct or reduce the rate of blood flow in the peripheral vasculature. The device is not intended for use in the neurovasculature, coronary vasculature, or pulmonary vasculature.

Contraindications

The NED coil is contraindicated for use in patients with known hypersensitivity to any component of the device. The device should not be used in vessels where the diameter exceeds the specified treatment range for the selected coil model.

Warnings

The NED coil should only be used by physicians trained in interventional radiology or vascular interventional procedures. Improper deployment technique may result in coil migration, non-target embolization, or incomplete vessel occlusion. Do not re-sterilize or reuse the device. The device is provided sterile for single use only.

Precautions

Careful patient selection and procedural planning are essential. The physician should verify vessel diameter compatibility with the selected coil model prior to deployment. Post-procedural imaging should be performed to confirm adequate embolization. Patients should be monitored for signs of non-target embolization or other complications.

Potential adverse events

Potential adverse events associated with peripheral embolization procedures may include, but are not limited to: vessel perforation or dissection, non-target embolization, coil migration, incomplete occlusion, post-embolization syndrome (pain, fever, nausea), infection, hematoma at the access site, allergic reaction, and vascular spasm.
